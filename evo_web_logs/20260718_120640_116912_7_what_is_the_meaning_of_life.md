## Direct Answer

The question "What is the meaning of life?" has no single answer — it is a foundational philosophical question that different frameworks answer in radically different ways. The answers fall into five broad categories:

1. **Objective intrinsic meaning** (Aristotelianism, Stoicism): Life's meaning is discovered, not created — it consists in fulfilling one's nature through virtue, reason, and flourishing.

2. **Objective divine meaning** (Abrahamic theism): Meaning is bestowed by a transcendent creator — life's purpose is to know, love, and serve God.

3. **Subjective constructed meaning** (Existentialism, Absurdism, Secular humanism): There is no inherent meaning, but humans can create their own through authentic choice, passion, relationships, creativity, and the pursuit of justice.

4. **Transcendent meaning through liberation** (Buddhism): Life's meaning involves understanding suffering, transcending craving, and attaining liberation (nirvana).

5. **Meaning denied or dissolved** (Nihilism, Biological naturalism): Either life has no meaning whatsoever (nihilism), or the question itself rests on a category error — "meaning" is a property of minds and language, not of biological processes (naturalism).

**The central fault line** is between **meaning as discovered** (objective, waiting to be found) and **meaning as created** (subjective, constructed by individuals or cultures). Which answer you find most compelling depends on prior commitments about metaphysics, epistemology, and value theory — commitments that are themselves philosophically contested.
