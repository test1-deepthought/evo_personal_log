## Problem Model

Let $K/\mathbb{Q}$ be a finite field extension, $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = n$, and $\alpha \in K$ an arbitrary element.

**Definitions:**
- $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ is the smallest subfield of $K$ containing $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\alpha$
- $m_\alpha(X) \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (the monic irreducible polynomial with $m_\alpha(\alpha)=0$)
- $\deg(m_\alpha)$ is the degree of the minimal polynomial
- $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq K$ is a tower of field extensions

## Mathematical Argument

### Proof that $\alpha \in K$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$

Since $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = n$, $K$ is an $n$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space. Consider the $n+1$ elements $\{1, \alpha, \alpha^2, \dots, \alpha^n\} \subseteq K$. By a standard result in linear algebra, any set of $n+1$ vectors in an $n$-dimensional vector space is linearly dependent over $\mathbb{Q}$. Hence there exist $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{Q}$, not all zero, such that

$$\sum_{i=0}^{n} a_i \alpha^i = 0_K.$$

Let $p(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} a_i X^i \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$. Then $p \neq 0$ and $p(\alpha) = 0$, so $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$. The minimal polynomial $m_\alpha(X)$ is then defined as the monic irreducible polynomial of smallest degree with $m_\alpha(\alpha) = 0$.

### Proof that $\deg(m_\alpha) = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]$

Since $\alpha$ is algebraic, the evaluation homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Q}[X] \to \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ defined by $\varphi(f) = f(\alpha)$ has kernel $(m_\alpha(X))$, the principal ideal generated by $m_\alpha$. By the First Isomorphism Theorem,

$$\mathbb{Q}[X]/(m_\alpha(X)) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\alpha).$$

The quotient $\mathbb{Q}[X]/(m_\alpha(X))$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space of dimension $\deg(m_\alpha)$, with basis $\{1, X, \dots, X^{d-1}\}$ where $d = \deg(m_\alpha)$. Therefore $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = \deg(m_\alpha)$.

### Proof that $\deg(m_\alpha)$ divides $n = [K:\mathbb{Q}]$

We have the chain of field extensions $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq K$. Since $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = n$ is finite, both $[K:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)]$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]$ are finite. The tower law for finite field extensions states:

$$[K:\mathbb{Q}] = [K:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)] \cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}].$$

Substituting $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = \deg(m_\alpha)$ and $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = n$ gives:

$$n = \deg(m_\alpha) \cdot [K:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)].$$

Since $[K:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)]$ is a positive integer, $\deg(m_\alpha)$ divides $n$.

The phrase "if and only if $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq K$ satisfies the tower law" reflects the logical structure: the divisibility $\deg(m_\alpha) \mid n$ holds **because** the tower law applies to the chain $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq K$. Since the tower law always holds for finite extensions, $\deg(m_\alpha)$ always divides $n$ for any $\alpha \in K$.

### Example where $\deg(m_\alpha) < n$

Take $\alpha = \omega \in K$. Then $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$, and $X^2 + X + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (discriminant $-3$, not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$). So $m_\omega(X) = X^2 + X + 1$ with $\deg(m_\omega) = 2$, and $2 < 6$.

Alternatively, take $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{2} \in K$. Then $m_\alpha(X) = X^3 - 2$ with $\deg = 3$, and $3 < 6$.

Another classic example: $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$ has $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 4$. Take $\alpha = \sqrt{2}$. Then $m_\alpha(X) = X^2 - 2$, $\deg = 2$, and $2 < 4$.

The condition $\deg(m_\alpha) < n$ occurs precisely when $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ is a proper subfield of $K$, i.e., when $[K:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)] > 1$, which happens whenever $\alpha$ does not generate the entire extension.
