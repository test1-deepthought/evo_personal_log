## Direct Answer

The question "What is the meaning of life?" does not admit a single, universally valid answer. Instead, the Prolog analysis reveals that different foundational assumptions lead to **10 distinct conclusions**, each internally coherent within its own worldview but mutually incompatible with others. The meaning of life is **framework-dependent**: it can be divine purpose, enlightenment, subjective construction, human flourishing, wellbeing-maximization, virtuous living, nothingness, radical self-creation, pluralistic integration, or a multidimensional construct — depending entirely on which metaphysical and axiological premises one accepts.

The most **assumption-robust** conclusions — those that survive the removal of the most assumptions — are:

1. **Living virtuously** (`meaning_is_living_virtuously`) — requires only that virtue is intrinsically valuable, an assumption compatible with theism, atheism, Buddhism, and Stoicism alike.
2. **Meaning has multiple dimensions** (`meaning_includes_multiple_dimensions`) — requires only that we distinguish purpose, value, and coherence as distinct aspects of meaning, which is a conceptual rather than metaphysical claim.

These two conclusions are the only ones that **transcend** the theist/atheist divide and the discovered/constructed dichotomy.
