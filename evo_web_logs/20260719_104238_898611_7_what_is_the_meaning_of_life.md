## Direct Answer

The question "What is the meaning of life?" is a classic philosophical question that has been debated for millennia. There is no single, universally accepted answer. Different frameworks offer different responses:

- **Religious perspectives**: Life's meaning derives from serving God, fulfilling divine purpose, or achieving salvation/enlightenment.
- **Philosophical perspectives**: 
  - Existentialism (Sartre, Camus): Life has no inherent meaning — we create our own meaning through choices and actions.
  - Absurdism (Camus): Life is inherently meaningless, but we can embrace this absurdity and live with passion.
  - Stoicism: Meaning comes from living virtuously in accordance with nature/reason.
  - Utilitarianism: Meaning comes from maximizing happiness and reducing suffering.
- **Scientific/biological perspective**: Life's "purpose" is reproduction and survival — passing genetic information to the next generation.
- **Humanistic psychology** (Frankl, Rogers): Meaning is found through love, work, suffering with dignity, and self-actualization.

Ultimately, the answer is subjective and personal — each individual must determine what gives their own life meaning.
